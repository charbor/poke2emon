Key Findings

19 of 35 species (54%) achieved 100% accuracy. The scanner is reliable for visually distinctive species: large mammals (bear, deer, pronghorn), colorful birds (eagle, meadowlark, hummingbird), and unique plants (columbine, spruce).

Snake identification is the weakest link. Bull Snake and Prairie Rattlesnake are confused bidirectionally (33% each). Key distinguishing features (pupil shape, head width, rattle) are often not visible in field photos. The Gopher Snake confuser also gets matched as Rattlesnake.

Mimicry defeats the scanner. Viceroy Butterfly (evolved to look like Monarch), Grizzly Bear (similar to Black Bear), and White-tailed Deer (similar to Mule Deer) all have 0% rejection rates. The distinguishing features (hindwing line, shoulder hump, antler pattern) may require higher-resolution photos than typical field shots.

Miller Moth is unidentifiable at 0% accuracy. These small brown moths look too generic for reliable photo identification. Consider removing from the scanner or accepting genus-level matches.

Next Steps

1. Run full eval (10 images per species, 450 total) for statistically significant results.
2. Add reference images to the prompt for the 5 most-confused species pairs.
3. Test with phone camera photos (current eval uses iNaturalist research-grade images).
4. Consider a two-pass approach: Claude for initial classification, then a specialized model for confusable pairs.
5. Evaluate BioCLIP 2 as an alternative for offline identification.
